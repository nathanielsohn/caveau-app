Samuel/Nate, (From Claude research)

Following up on our conversation — here's how I'm thinking about the bottle tracking system and what it means operationally for the floor.

The short version: no QR stickers. Ever. A sticker on a $10,000 bottle of Screaming Eagle risks damaging the label and signals that someone has handled and modified the bottle post-production. Auction houses notice. It's a non-starter.

Instead, we're going with NFC — Near Field Communication. It's the same technology used by Chateau Le Pin and other top Bordeaux estates to authenticate and track their bottles. A phone tap to the tag pulls up the full Caveau record for that bottle instantly. No app required on the buyer's end. No visible mark on the bottle.

Here's how it works at intake:

**Trophy bottles ($1,000+)** — we apply an NFC capsule tag directly under or over the foil at the top of the bottle. It's invisible. Doesn't touch the label. The bottle looks exactly as it did when it arrived. When a member or buyer taps their phone to the capsule, it pulls up the bottle's complete Caveau Custody & Condition record — purchase history, storage data, custody chain.

**Standard cellar bottles (under $1,000)** — we put a branded Caveau navy neck collar on the bottle with an NFC chip embedded in it. This one is visible and intentionally so. It's a signal that the bottle is under Caveau management. We'll design it to be beautiful — navy, gold CAVEAU wordmark, fine gold border lines top and bottom. Something a member sees in their cellar and feels good about.

Both tag types link to the same platform record and scan identically in the app. The difference is just presentation — calibrated to the value of the bottle.

Why this matters operationally: most wine storage competitors track collections at the case level. We track at the bottle level, from the day it enters custody. That granularity is what makes our Custody & Condition Report credible when a member goes to sell — Christie's and Sotheby's want an unbroken record for every bottle, not a case summary.

For you on the floor, the intake process is straightforward — tag the bottle, photograph it, assign it to the member's portfolio in the app. The system does the rest.
